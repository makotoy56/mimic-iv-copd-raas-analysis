RAAS Blockers and In-Hospital Mortality in ICU Admissions With COPD

Author

Makoto Yoshida, PhD

Updated 2026-11-18 from analysis version admission_age_elapsed_time_los_v2. Generated from aggregate current results.

1 Executive summary

In 11,964 COPD ICU admissions, pre-ICU RAAS exposure was associated with lower observed in-hospital mortality hazard in the refined age/sex-adjusted, era-stratified Cox model (HR 0.732 (0.592–0.904); p=0.00383). The exploratory first-day-SOFA core specification had an attenuated estimate (HR 0.833 (0.673–1.030); p=0.09122). These estimates are observational associations and cannot establish a treatment effect.

2 Data source and cohort

MIMIC-IV v3.1 admissions with the existing ICD-9/10 COPD definition were anchored to the first ICU stay within each hospital admission. Patients may contribute repeated admissions. Adult selection uses anchor_age + admission_year - anchor_year >= 18. The same admission-age estimate is preserved through baseline and outcome construction. anchor_age=91 denotes an aggregated age group above 89 in the anchor year, so year adjustment does not recover an exact age.

4 Exposure, outcome, and methods

Exposure flags identify inpatient prescription records in the same hospital admission with starttime <= intime. They do not verify actual administration, continued treatment at ICU entry, adherence or concurrent ACEi/ARB administration. Both positive class flags mean qualifying records for both classes; they do not establish simultaneous treatment. Neither means no qualifying prescription record for either class, not proven absence of all medication use.

Follow-up begins at ICU admission and ends at recorded in-hospital death or live discharge. Discharge time is used for death only when death time is missing, with an explicit fallback flag. All intervals, including ICU and hospital LOS, are calculated from DATETIME microsecond differences divided by 86,400,000,000. Death/discharge timestamps, event values, positive follow-up, and downstream lineage are validated before analysis.

There were 2 fallback deaths. In the original main analysis, live discharge is censored and Cox intervals use ordinary admission-level inference without patient clustering or frailty. Supplementary patient-cluster and competing-event results are reported separately below. Primary 04a adjusts for age and sex and stratifies by anchor year group. Core 04c additionally adjusts for SOFA, CHF, CKD and diabetes. ICU sensitivity models add pooled ICU types; penalties remain 0.1 for core and 0.01 for ICU. Original unpooled ICU estimates are retained in the archived age/time audit.

5 Timing of severity information

sofa_score comes from mimiciv_3_1_derived.first_day_sofa. Its published definition can use information through 24 hours after ICU entry, while follow-up starts at ICU entry. All SOFA-containing core, ICU-adjusted and CHF/SOFA-stratified models in the original ICU-entry workflow are therefore exploratory sensitivity analyses that can use post-start information, not baseline-only severity adjustment. Temporal leakage or adjustment for post-exposure information may affect interpretation; its impact has not been quantified here. The primary age/sex model with era strata is unchanged. A separate exploratory 24-hour landmark analysis is reported in the supplementary results; pre-ICU severity reconstruction remains unperformed.

Published first-day SOFA definition. The deployed derived-table definition and individual measurement times were not inspected in this documentation audit. Separate reanalysis options.

6 Kaplan-Meier results

The two-group log-rank statistic was 8.207, p=0.00417. These are unadjusted survival estimates under live-discharge censoring, not competing-risk-adjusted hospital death probabilities.

How to read

  • The horizontal axis is elapsed days since ICU entry; the vertical axis is the unadjusted Kaplan–Meier survival estimate. Blue represents qualifying pre-ICU RAAS orders and orange represents no qualifying orders. Shading shows pointwise 95% confidence intervals.
  • Live discharge is censored. Consequently, one minus this curve is not the observed cumulative probability of hospital death when discharge is a competing event; use the separate CIF figure for that question.

What the results show

  • The overall two-group log-rank p-value is 0.00417. It compares the unadjusted survival experience over follow-up, rather than testing every plotted day or controlling for age, severity and other confounding.

Interpretation limits

  • Wider intervals in the late tail indicate less precise estimates. Visual separation is an observational association and does not establish a medication benefit. The curve is not an adjusted Cox prediction.

8 Subclass comparison

The ACEi-only-order, ARB-only-order and neither-order analysis includes 11,951 admissions. Its global three-group log-rank statistic is 9.124, p=0.01044. Records with both class flags positive are excluded. Each coefficient compares a subclass with neither qualifying order.

ACEi-only and ARB-only prescription-order groups are each compared with neither qualifying order. Records with both class flags positive are excluded from this subclass analysis. The main analysis reports no direct ACEi-versus-ARB contrast or interaction test. Different point estimates, or one p-value below 0.05 and the other above it, do not establish a difference between the classes.

Interpretation limits

  • Different point estimates or p-value thresholds do not establish a difference between classes. This plot contains no direct ACEi-versus-ARB contrast and does not establish superiority or equivalence.

10 Supplementary Sensitivity Analyses

These post hoc analyses were added after the main analysis. They use the same corrected cohort and prescription-order exposure definition; the original main estimates remain unchanged. The selected aggregate results are bound to the main reference and verified local run by hashes. Full methods and notebook links are in the supplement guide.

10.1 Patient-cluster inference

The 11,964 admissions represent 9,157 patients; 1,656 patients contributed 4,463 admissions across repeated hospitalizations. Unpenalized age/sex/era and SOFA-core Cox models retain their original complete cases, covariates and Efron ties. Patient-level HC0 sandwich inference sums tie-aware scores across all admissions of each patient. Point estimates are shared by the two variance methods; intervals and two-sided normal Wald p-values can change. No finite-cluster correction or frailty model is used.

10.2 Death and live discharge as competing events

A two-cause Aalen-Johansen estimate treats recorded in-hospital death and live discharge as competing endpoints. Curves are unadjusted admission-level cumulative incidence through day 28. Pointwise 95% percentile intervals use 1,000 patient-cluster bootstrap samples, carrying all admissions and exposure groups of each sampled patient together, with fixed patient ordering and seed 20261118. Tied endpoints use a shared pre-event risk set without jitter. The original discharge-time proxy is retained for the two deaths with missing death timestamps. These intervals are not simultaneous confidence bands.

10.3 A separate 24-hour severity landmark

Follow-up starts 24 hours after the original ICU entry among admissions alive and still hospitalized beyond that point. Exclusions were 254 deaths and 178 live discharges at or before 24 hours, plus 2 remaining deaths with unknown death times. This left 11,530 eligible admissions; 0 had missing core covariates. Both models use the same complete cases, unchanged pre-ICU prescription-order classification, era strata, and patient-cluster Efron inference; one day is subtracted from eligible follow-up.

10.4 Validation and interpretation

Ordinary estimates were checked against the saved main reference before these results were saved. Synthetic tied and untied calculations matched R survival for Cox coefficients, ordinary and patient-cluster covariance, and Aalen-Johansen probabilities. That synthetic comparison is not an independent clinical refit of these clustered estimates. The supplement validation record documents the checks and remaining limitations. All three analyses are reported regardless of significance; none establishes a causal treatment effect.

11 Reproducibility checks

Six unpenalized Cox specifications (40 coefficients) were independently recalculated with statsmodels PHReg using matched complete cases, era/additional strata and Efron ties. Coefficients, HRs, confidence-interval endpoints and p-values matched the lifelines reference within an absolute tolerance of 1e-4. The saved comparison is bound to this exact aggregate analysis snapshot by its reference hash. See the calculation record and the scope and limitations.

This checks a separate statistical implementation on the same corrected model inputs. Source-SQL extraction is evaluated separately. Penalized fits, PH diagnostics, patient clustering, competing risks and causal validity are not established by this numerical comparison.

The reproducible workflow covers authorized MIMIC source access, version-controlled BigQuery extraction and cohort construction, explicit model-column retrieval, Python modeling, independent numerical comparison, figure generation and Quarto reporting. Only aggregate results are published; no external analysis-service upload is part of this workflow. See the full reproduction steps.

12 Limitations

These findings cannot establish causality. Confounding by indication, unmeasured severity, exposure misclassification and a single health system limit interpretation. Age is approximate, especially in the aggregated oldest group. The original main estimates retain ordinary admission-level inference and discharge censoring; supplementary analyses report patient-cluster inference and competing-event cumulative incidence. Rare ICU pooling and PH-responsive strata remain exploratory sensitivity choices. First-day SOFA may include post-start information; these models do not isolate baseline confounding. No direct between-class contrast or verified medication-administration comparison is reported.
