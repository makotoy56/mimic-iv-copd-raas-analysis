RAAS Blockers and In-Hospital Mortality in ICU Admissions With COPD

Author

Makoto Yoshida, PhD

Updated 2026-11-18 from analysis version admission_age_elapsed_time_los_v2. Generated from aggregate current results.

1 Executive summary

In 11,964 COPD ICU admissions, pre-ICU RAAS exposure was associated with lower observed in-hospital mortality hazard in the refined age/sex-adjusted, era-stratified Cox model (HR 0.732 (0.592–0.904); p=0.00383). Extended core adjustment attenuated the association (HR 0.833 (0.673–1.030); p=0.09122). These estimates are observational associations and cannot establish a treatment effect.

2 Data source and cohort

MIMIC-IV v3.1 admissions with the existing ICD-9/10 COPD definition were anchored to the first ICU stay within each hospital admission. Patients may contribute repeated admissions. Adult selection uses anchor_age + admission_year - anchor_year >= 18. The same admission-age estimate is preserved through baseline and outcome construction. anchor_age=91 denotes an aggregated age group above 89 in the anchor year, so year adjustment does not recover an exact age.

3 Exposure, outcome, and methods

Exposure is based on inpatient ACEi/ARB prescription orders before or at ICU admission. It does not directly measure outpatient chronic use or adherence. Follow-up begins at ICU admission and ends at recorded in-hospital death or live discharge. Discharge time is used for death only when death time is missing, with an explicit fallback flag. All intervals, including ICU and hospital LOS, are calculated from DATETIME microsecond differences divided by 86,400,000,000. Death/discharge timestamps, event values, positive follow-up, and downstream lineage are validated before analysis.

There were 2 fallback deaths. Live discharge is censored; a competing-risk framework is not implemented. Cox models use admission-level records without patient clustering or frailty. Primary 04a adjusts for age and sex and stratifies by anchor year group. Core 04c additionally adjusts for SOFA, CHF, CKD and diabetes. ICU sensitivity models add pooled ICU types; penalties remain 0.1 for core and 0.01 for ICU. Original unpooled ICU estimates are retained in the archived age/time audit.

4 Kaplan-Meier results

The two-group log-rank statistic was 8.207, p=0.00417. These are unadjusted survival estimates under live-discharge censoring, not competing-risk-adjusted hospital death probabilities.

6 Subclass comparison

The monotherapy/neither analysis includes 11,951 admissions. Its global three-group log-rank statistic is 9.124, p=0.01044. Dual-exposure admissions are excluded. Each coefficient compares a subclass with neither exposure; this is not a formal ACEi-versus-ARB interaction test.

7 PH diagnostics and interpretation

Primary refined rank PH p-values: age 0.79803, sex 0.32498, RAAS 0.72064. Core and ICU models still require diagnostic interpretation. The current results summary records every covariate-level PH test and warning. Exploratory CHF/SOFA-stratified models allow different baseline hazards; they supplement the primary model rather than redefine it.

For full diagnostics, sparse-strata limitations and rare-category pooling, see current results. The approach follows lifelines documentation on stratification. A non-significant diagnostic does not prove PH, and additional stratification changes the adjustment structure.

8 Reproducibility checks

Six unpenalized Cox specifications (40 coefficients) were independently recalculated with statsmodels PHReg using matched complete cases, era/additional strata and Efron ties. Coefficients, HRs, confidence-interval endpoints and p-values matched the lifelines reference within an absolute tolerance of 1e-4. The saved comparison is bound to this exact aggregate analysis snapshot by its reference hash. See the calculation record and the scope and limitations.

This checks a separate statistical implementation on the same corrected model inputs. Source-SQL extraction is evaluated separately. Penalized fits, PH diagnostics, patient clustering, competing risks and causal validity are not established by this numerical comparison.

The reproducible workflow covers authorized MIMIC source access, version-controlled BigQuery extraction and cohort construction, explicit model-column retrieval, Python modeling, independent numerical comparison, figure generation and Quarto reporting. Only aggregate results are published; no external analysis-service upload is part of this workflow. See the full reproduction steps.

9 Limitations

These findings cannot establish causality. Confounding by indication, unmeasured severity, exposure misclassification and a single health system limit interpretation. Age is approximate, especially in the aggregated oldest group. Repeated admissions are not clustered; live discharge remains censoring; rare ICU pooling and PH-responsive strata are exploratory sensitivity choices.
